Te Tiriti o Waitangi: What Internationally Qualified Nurses Need to Know

April 14, 2026 Cultural Safety 6 min read

Te Tiriti o Waitangi (the Treaty of Waitangi) is New Zealand’s founding document and forms the cornerstone of culturally safe nursing practice in Aotearoa New Zealand. For internationally qualified nurses (IQNs) aiming to register and work in New Zealand, a clear understanding of Te Tiriti o Waitangi is essential.

Understanding and applying the principles of Te Tiriti o Waitangi is not only a registration requirement but also a fundamental part of delivering equitable, respectful, and safe care to Māori and all New Zealanders.

What is Te Tiriti o Waitangi?

Te Tiriti o Waitangi was signed in 1840 between the British Crown and Māori chiefs. It is a living document that outlines the relationship between the Crown and Māori. In modern New Zealand healthcare, it serves as the foundation for partnership, protection, and participation in all aspects of service delivery.

Key Principles of Te Tiriti o Waitangi in Nursing

The Nursing Council of New Zealand expects all nurses to demonstrate understanding and application of the following core principles:

  • Partnership (Mahitahi): Working collaboratively with Māori, their whānau, and communities.
  • Protection (Kaitiaki): Actively protecting Māori health and wellbeing, including cultural values and practices.
  • Participation (Whakauru): Ensuring Māori have meaningful involvement in decisions affecting their health.
  • Equity (Tika): Reducing health disparities and providing fair access to quality healthcare.

Why is Te Tiriti o Waitangi Important for IQNs?

The Nursing Council of New Zealand (NCNZ) has made cultural safety and Te Tiriti o Waitangi a core competency. Internationally qualified nurses must show they can:

  • Deliver culturally safe care
  • Understand the impact of colonisation on Māori health
  • Apply Treaty principles in everyday clinical practice
  • Complete the mandatory “Welcome to the Aotearoa New Zealand Programme”
