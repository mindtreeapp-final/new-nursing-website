Understanding OSCE: A Comprehensive Guide for Internationally Qualified Nurses

April 14, 2026 OSCE Training 7 min read

Are you an internationally qualified nurse considering registration in New Zealand? The process may seem daunting, but fear not — we're here to guide you through it.

The Nursing Council of New Zealand has introduced changes to the competence assessment requirements, including the implementation of the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE). Let's delve into everything you need to know about OSCE and the registration process.

OSCE is now a vital part of the competence assessment for internationally qualified nurses (IQNs) seeking registration in Aotearoa New Zealand.

What's Changed?

The Nursing Council has revamped the competence assessment process for internationally qualified nurses seeking registration in New Zealand. This change aims to ensure a standardized and fair evaluation of nursing competency, aligning with international standards and prioritizing public safety.

The introduction of OSCE as part of the assessment process signifies a shift towards objective evaluation and culturally safe practice.

Understanding OSCE

OSCE, or Objective Structured Clinical Examination, is a crucial component of the competence assessment process. It consists of a two-day orientation and preparation course followed by a three-hour clinical examination.

Unlike traditional exams, OSCE evaluates not only theoretical knowledge but also practical skills and clinical reasoning in a simulated clinical environment.

Why OSCE?

OSCE offers several important benefits:

  • Objective Evaluation: OSCE provides an objective and standardized method for assessing clinical competence, ensuring fairness and consistency across all candidates.
  • Culturally Safe Practice: In line with Te Tiriti o Waitangi principles, OSCE evaluates candidates' ability to practice culturally safe nursing care, reflecting New Zealand’s unique healthcare context.
  • Public Safety: By assessing practical skills and clinical reasoning, OSCE helps safeguard public health by ensuring that registered nurses possess the necessary competencies to deliver safe and effective care.

The Registration Process

Here’s a step-by-step overview of the registration process:

  • Document Verification: Before applying for registration, internationally qualified nurses must have their documents verified and authenticated by CGFNS (now TruMerit).
  • Welcome to the Aotearoa New Zealand Programme: Completion of this program, consisting of two free online courses, is mandatory for all applicants. It provides an introduction to culturally safe nursing practice in New Zealand and Te Tiriti o Waitangi principles.
  • English Language Assessment: There are no changes to the current English language assessment requirements. Candidates must meet the specified language proficiency standards.
