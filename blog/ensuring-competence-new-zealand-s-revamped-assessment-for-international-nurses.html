Ensuring Competence: New Zealand's Revamped Assessment for International Nurses

April 14, 2026 New Zealand Registration 7 min read

In the ever-evolving landscape of healthcare, New Zealand has taken a significant step to ensure the highest standards of nursing care. The Nursing Council of New Zealand has introduced a new competence assessment process for internationally qualified nurses (IQNs) seeking to practice in the country.

The revamped assessment focuses on public safety, consistency, and objectivity — directly testing theoretical knowledge, clinical skills, and cultural competence.

Why Was the Assessment Revamped?

The decision to revamp the assessment system was driven by a commitment to public safety and a desire for a consistent and objective approach to evaluating nursing competence. The previous system lacked uniformity, with competence assessment programs varying across different regions.

The new process aims to directly test a nurse’s theoretical knowledge, clinical skills, and cultural competence, rather than relying solely on qualifications that may be outdated. This ensures that IQNs possess the necessary expertise to provide quality care in New Zealand’s unique healthcare environment.

What the Revamped Assessment Entails

Theoretical Examination

IQNs must pass an online theoretical exam that evaluates their conceptual nursing knowledge. This exam is conducted at accredited Pearson virtual exam centres, both within New Zealand and internationally.

Clinical Assessment

A two-day orientation and preparation course precedes an Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE). The OSCE is a three-hour practical assessment that tests clinical and professional skills, conducted at the Nurse Maude simulation and assessment centre in Christchurch.

Cultural Competence

To introduce IQNs to culturally safe nursing practices in New Zealand and the principles of Te Tiriti o Waitangi, they must complete an online “Welcome to Aotearoa” program. While serving as an introduction, this program is the starting point for a lifelong journey of cultural understanding that employers will continue to foster.

Transition Period

The previous competence assessment programs will run alongside the new process until early 2025, giving nurses adequate time to prepare.

The new assessment process aligns with international best practices. By directly testing nurses’ knowledge and skills, New Zealand is ensuring that its nursing workforce meets the highest standards.

As New Zealand’s healthcare demands continue to grow, the contribution of internationally qualified nurses remains invaluable.
